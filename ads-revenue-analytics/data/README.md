# Data

Synthetic, generated by [`generate_data.py`](generate_data.py). Seeded
(`SEED = 20260218`) so every figure in the analysis reproduces exactly.

| File | Rows | Grain |
|---|---|---|
| `sales_reps.csv` | 42 | one row per sales rep |
| `advertisers.csv` | 340 | one row per advertiser |
| `campaigns.csv` | 3,143 | one row per campaign |
| `daily_performance.csv` | 195,488 | one row per campaign per delivering day |

**Period:** 2024-07-01 → 2025-12-31 · **Spend:** $566.3M · **Attributed GMV:** $1.87B

## Schema

**`sales_reps.csv`** — `rep_id`, `rep_name`, `region`, `seniority`, `hire_date`

**`advertisers.csv`** — `advertiser_id`, `advertiser_name`, `region`,
`vertical`, `account_tier`, `signup_date`, `rep_id`, `churn_date`
(`churn_date` is NULL for accounts still active at the end of the window —
every retention query depends on that convention)

**`campaigns.csv`** — `campaign_id`, `advertiser_id`, `objective`,
`start_date`, `end_date`

**`daily_performance.csv`** — `date`, `campaign_id`, `advertiser_id`,
`impressions`, `clicks`, `conversions`, `spend_usd`, `attributed_gmv_usd`

> A campaign does **not** have a row for every day between its start and end
> dates — budget pacing and pauses mean some days do not deliver. Any query
> needing a continuous daily series must build a date spine rather than assume
> one row per campaign-day.

## Built-in business patterns

The generator plants six patterns for the analysis to find:

1. **Q4 seasonality** — November/December retail peak, January/February trough.
2. **Segment shock** — Southeast Asia Gaming declines from April 2025, decaying
   to ~22% of its prior level. Total revenue keeps growing, so this is only
   visible once revenue is decomposed by segment.
3. **Tiered churn** — SMB 82.5%, Mid-Market 44.4%, Enterprise 16.3%.
4. **Rep ramp** — new hires reach ~90% of tenured productivity by month 4.
5. **Book-size penalty** — retention degrades as a rep's book grows past ~8
   accounts.
6. **Under-invested vertical** — Beauty & Personal Care returns 4.75x ROAS on
   10.7% of spend.

## On getting the patterns to survive

Two versions of this generator produced findings that were not real, and the
fixes are documented in the code comments because the diagnosis is the
interesting part:

- **The segment shock was invisible, then inverted.** Southeast Asia Gaming
  started as ~5% of advertisers — too thin to carry a trend. Its monthly index
  swung `100 → 258 → 34 → (no data) → 25` on sampling noise alone. Worse, once
  campaigns were scattered randomly across each advertiser's lifetime, the
  number of active campaigns in a segment swung 2–3x month to month, and that
  composition noise made the segment appear to *grow* during the months its
  planted shock had cut delivery to 74%. Fixed by tilting each region's
  vertical mix realistically (SEA over-indexes on mobile gaming) and by
  modelling campaigns as continuous back-to-back "lanes" rather than random
  scatter.
- **The book-size effect rested on two reps.** Advertisers were assigned to
  reps uniformly, giving every rep ~8 accounts and far too narrow a spread to
  relate book size to retention. Fixed by drawing a per-rep capacity weight so
  books range from 1 to 45 accounts, and by replacing the hard threshold with
  a smooth hazard penalty that scales with book size.

Both were caught by checking whether the planted finding was actually present
before writing any analysis around it.
